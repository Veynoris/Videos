# EP07 — caption

One ant. One sentence. Ten things.

When Sulaymān's army reached the valley of the ants, one ant said:
«يَـٰٓأَيُّهَا ٱلنَّمۡلُ ٱدۡخُلُواْ مَسَٰكِنَكُمۡ لَا يَحۡطِمَنَّكُمۡ سُلَيۡمَٰنُ وَجُنُودُهُۥ وَهُمۡ لَا يَشۡعُرُونَ»
"O ants, enter your dwellings, that you not be crushed by Sulaymān and his soldiers while they perceive not." (an-Naml 27:18)

Scholars of Arabic rhetoric counted ten things in it:
1. يا — she called out
2. أيها — she alerted
3. النمل — she named them
4. ادخلوا — she commanded
5. مساكنكم — she specified where
6. لا يحطمنكم — she warned
7. سليمان — she singled out the king
8. وجنوده — she included his army
9. وهم — she pointed to them
10. لا يشعرون — she excused them

She warned her people about the army — and in the same breath excused it: "while they perceive not."

And Sulaymān smiled at her words, and said: "My Lord, enable me to be grateful for Your favor." (27:19)

(Ṣafwat al-Tafāsīr, on 27:18)
