# Quran Decoding — Episode 02 (proposal): "One sentence"

**Status:** awaiting approval. Nothing has been rendered.

## 1. The claim

| Layer | Content | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Qur'an | Sūrat al-Masad (111) names a living man, Abū Lahab, the Prophet's ﷺ uncle. It declares that he will burn in a blazing Fire, and his wife with him. | Text: KFGQPC Hafs |
| Context | The surah came down when Abū Lahab cursed the Prophet ﷺ at Mount Ṣafā, after "And warn your nearest kin" (26:214) was revealed. | **Sahih al-Bukhārī 4971** (Ibn ʿAbbās), with parallels 4972 and 4973 |
| History | Abū Lahab died about a week after the Battle of Badr (Ramaḍān 2 AH / March 624 CE) as a disbeliever, and so did his wife. That is roughly **ten years** after the surah. | Ibn Isḥāq / Ibn Hishām; Ibn Kathīr, *al-Bidāya wa-l-Nihāya* (the chapter on news of Badr reaching Mecca); Dār al-Iftāʾ (Egypt). ⚠ Check page numbers in print. |
| Scholarly point | Ibn Kathīr, in his tafsir of al-Masad, reports that the scholars said this surah is **"a clear miracle and evident proof of prophethood."** It foretold that both would die without faith, and neither of them believed, outwardly or inwardly. | Found via search extracts (quran.ksu.edu.sa). ⚠ Confirm the wording on the page. |

**What the video will not say:**
- It won't invent dialogue.
- It won't depict the Prophet ﷺ, Companions or Abū Lahab. Only text, landscape and light appear.
- It won't claim exact dates. The timing is "about ten years", because the year of the Ṣafā event is approximate (around the 3rd–4th year of prophethood).

## 2. Hook (first 3 seconds)

> **"He had ten years to prove the Qur'an wrong."**
> *"All he needed was one sentence."*

## 3. On-screen script (no voiceover, halal sound design only)

1. **Hook:** "He had ten years to prove the Qur'an wrong." → "All he needed was one sentence."
2. "Mecca. A man named Abū Lahab — the Prophet's own uncle." (`SAHIH AL-BUKHĀRĪ 4971`)
3. At Mount Ṣafā he cursed the Prophet ﷺ. The answer came as a surah: the al-Masad card, 111:1–3 in Arabic with Sahih International, the name "Abū Lahab" in gold.
4. "It said he would die a disbeliever — and his wife too." (111:3–5)
5. "Now think." → "All he had to do was say: *'I believe.'* Even falsely. Even once." → "And the prophecy would collapse."
6. A ten-year ring of days drains: around 3,650 days, each one a chance. "Every day for ten years, he could have said it."
7. "He never did." Badr, 2 AH, then his death, as a disbeliever. His wife too.
8. Ibn Kathīr: "Scholars called this surah 'a clear miracle and evident proof of prophethood.'"
9. **Close:** "A prophecy that one sentence could have broken." → "*It never broke.*" Sign-off: `AL-MASAD · 111`

Runtime: about 60–65 s.

## 4. Visual language (same series identity as EP01)

- **Palette and type:** the same navy background, Newsreader serif, tracked monospace labels and KFGQPC Arabic. Fire is a deep ember orange (new to this episode). Faith is the established gold. The silver moon tone is not used here.
- **Hook:** the night skyline of Mecca's mountains. A single line of text appears, then a giant ring clock of 10 years whose days burn away like embers.
- **Ṣafā:** a mountain silhouette at dawn, with a gold glow on the summit and no figures.
- **Al-Masad card:** the 3D tilt-up glass card with the rosette behind it. The name أَبِي لَهَبٍ is lit in ember.
- **"I believe":** the two words type out in gold, flicker, and then never complete. A cursor blinks over empty space for ten years.
- **The ten-year ring:** 3,650 ember dots extinguish one by one, with a counter. Each year is marked "1 … 10".
- **Close:** the ring goes dark, and one line of light remains.

## 5. Sound (halal policy unchanged)

Embers crackle, wind over the mountains, stone thuds, a heartbeat-like low thud for the "one sentence" moment, sand and air swells, and a clock tick for the years. No music, no instruments, no bells, no recitation.

## 6. Open checks before publishing

1. The Ibn Kathīr wording in his tafsir of al-Masad, which I found only through search.
2. The account of Abū Lahab's death after Badr, in Ibn Hishām or *al-Bidāya wa-l-Nihāya*, with page numbers.
3. Optional, stronger detail, unverified: two of Abū Lahab's sons, ʿUtba and Muʿattib, accepted Islam at the conquest of Mecca. This shows faith was always open to his family. It stays out of the video unless verified.
