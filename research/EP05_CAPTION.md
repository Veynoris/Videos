# EP05 — caption

The word qamīṣ — shirt — appears six times in the Qur'an. Every one of them is in Sūrat Yūsuf.

Three shirts. Three verdicts.

I. The brothers brought his shirt "with false blood" (12:18) and said a wolf had eaten him. Ibn Kathīr reports from Ibn ʿAbbās that Yaʿqūb examined it and found it untorn — a wolf that eats a boy and leaves his shirt whole? He said: "Rather, your souls have enticed you to something."

II. Years later in Egypt, a shirt was torn as they raced to the door. A witness set the test: torn from the front, she is truthful; torn from the back, he is truthful. It was torn from the back. (12:25–28)

III. Yūsuf sent his shirt: "Cast it over my father's face." As the caravan left Egypt, Yaʿqūb said, "I find the scent of Yūsuf." It was cast over his face — and he could see again. (12:93–96)

One shirt brought the grief. Another brought the cure.
And in between, Yaʿqūb said the same words twice — at the first loss and at the second: فَصَبۡرٞ جَمِيلٞ, beautiful patience. (12:18, 12:83)
